# Roval cockpit Wahoo out-front mount

Replaces the Roval out-front arm + Bar Fly Wahoo puck (tabs broken, taped) + under-puck
GoPro on a one-piece Roval bar/stem. One printed part:

- **Back wall** seats on the bar's FRONT face; one bolt goes horizontally straight back into
  it through a vertical slot (+-1.5 up/down). Bolt head sits in the throat of a horizontal
  key slot, so a straight hex key reaches it from the front.
- **Level flat-oval arm** (15-17 wide x 6.6 tall), top flush with the Wahoo seat, flaring
  tangent into the receiver disc in plan.
- **Integrated Wahoo receiver**, the real geometry: `extract_receiver.py` cuts the
  quarter-turn ring (lip, tab slots, detent fingers) out of Wahoo's Bolt 2 cradle
  `ref/Mount_base-bolt2_v1.stl` (an STL of `Mount_base-bolt2` from Wahoo's MIT-licensed
  [ELEMNT-Mount](https://github.com/WahooFitness/ELEMNT-Mount) repo) into `ref/wahoo_receiver.stl`,
  and the `.scad` grafts it onto the head. Do not re-model the receiver by hand.
- **GoPro 3-prong tangs** form the lower jaw of the root block (under the key slot), in the
  bar's wake, instead of hanging under the computer.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `roval_wahoo_mount.scad` | the model; `part=` `"mount"` (assembled), `"arm"`, `"insert"` (the Wahoo receiver, also the twist-lock test piece), `"bar"` |
| `extract_receiver.py` | re-cut the receiver graft from the Wahoo STL (`python3 extract_receiver.py`, from this dir) |
| `ref/` | the extracted receiver graft (Wahoo's design, MIT: `ref/WAHOO-LICENSE.txt`). The full cradle STL isn't included; export `BOLT2_MOUNT/Mount_base-bolt2.STEP` from Wahoo's repo to STL to re-cut it |
| `out/` | built STLs |

Build: `python3.9 build_3mf.py` (from the repo root: `roval-wahoo-mount/build_3mf.py`) rebuilds
`out/mount.stl` and writes `out/roval_wahoo_mount.3mf`. The bar section comes from
`section/bar_profile.scad`, generated by `section/build_profile.py` from the photo traces.

## Underside variant "flat" (2026-11-25, the designer: the gradual slope prints jagged)

- `underside = "slope"` (default, v18) | `"flat"`. **Flat:** z 0.8 from the bar through the GoPro to
  x 36.4, then a 30° step up to the ring's flat (z 6.2) at x 45.75. Build it with
  `build_3mf.py flat` → `out/roval_wahoo_mount_flat.3mf` and `out/mount_flat.stl` (side by side with
  `out/mount.stl`).
- **Why 0.8:** the bar bolt. The slot's lowest edge is at ~z 2.2, and the head's lowest seat is at 0.77
  (`slot_down` 0.75). The flat leaves 1.38 under the slot (the slope left 1.94). the designer chose a single flat
  at 0.8 over two flats (0.8 under the bolt, 3.4 across the GoPro).
- **GoPro walls (tang) depth:**

  | | slot back end | screw | slot front end |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | slope | 10.44 | 9.34 | 8.23 |
  | flat | **11.6** | **11.6** | **11.6** |
  | difference | **+1.16** | **+2.27** | **+3.37** |

  From the screw axis down to the underside is 7.1 mm in flat, against 5.9 → 3.7 on the slope (the
  slope is proven to fit the accessory and light). **The flat needs the accessory to clear 7.1 mm below
  its screw-hole centre. Not checked yet.**
- Bar end 11.6 tall (slope: 13.07). Arm 7.45 cm³ vs 6.60. 12.2 g sliced (slope 11.5), no support.

## Locating ridges replace the tongue (2026-11-23, the designer)

- the designer wanted the underside a clean circle, and guidance on the way in instead of turning the insert
  at the end to line the tab up. So the flange is a **plain disc** (no tongue), and two **vertical
  45° triangular ridges** on the insert's body (forward and back) run in **V-grooves up the ring's
  bore**.
- **Keyed:** the back ridge (toward the bar) is 1.5 high, the front 0.8. The insert only goes in one
  way round, because the receiver isn't symmetric: rotated 180° the meshes collide (2.9 mm³). For
  identical ridges, set `ridge_h_back = ridge_h_front`.
- **Ridges:** z 7.8–11.8, with a 45° roof. They engage after 1.6 mm of insertion.
- **Grooves:** flank clearance 0.1, 0.3 extra depth at the tip, and a 0.4 flared mouth at the
  counterbore ceiling. They stop at z 12.0, 2 layers under the top: the ring's top and underside
  were checked by ray-cast and both stay clean.
- With no tongue, the flat underside only has to start at the counterbore (`x_flat`), so the ring
  goes back to minimum depth: `head_bot` 6.2, insert 7.2 tall, floor 0.55. The arm changes, so it
  needs a reprint along with the 3.05 GoPro slots.
- Arm 7.03 cm³, insert 3.33; overlap 0 as assembled. Sliced: 11.9 g together (insert alone 4.1 g),
  no support. **Not printed yet.**

## Pins removed (2026-11-23, the designer)

- The insert's two d2.4 × 2 locating pins snapped off easily: thin vertical pins on a part printed
  flange-down break across the layer lines. the designer: the tongue in its counterbore is locator enough;
  the pins added nothing. They're gone from the insert and the coupons, and their holes are gone from
  the arm (a pinless insert still fits an older arm with holes). `pin_*` params removed.
- Insert 3.74 cm³, 4.6 g; overlap 0. **Lesson:** don't put small vertical pins on a part when they
  stand up off the layer lines; let a keyed shape (tongue, flat) do the locating.

## GoPro slots 3.15 → 3.05 (2026-11-23, the designer: "feels too sloppy")

- The 3.15 coupon was picked by hand, but the mount on the bike felt sloppy. The arm mesh was
  verified to match the coupon: slots 3.15, tang 3.1, walls 3.3. At the designer's call: **`gp_slot_w`
  3.05** (tang unchanged at 3.1; walls now 3.4; channel 9.2; 1.9 from the nut recess to the slot).
  Measured on the new mesh: slots 3.050. **Lesson:** a coupon pushed on by hand reads looser-fit
  as fine; for a clamped joint that takes vibration, pick the tightest coupon that still goes on.
  **Not tested yet.**

## Insert fit (2026-11-23): insert grown 0.10, ring unchanged (the designer: "insert is very sloppy")

- **Print result (v14–19 ring + insert):** the 2 pins were tight, but the insert's body and flange
  were sloppy in the ring. The model had 0.15 radial clearance on all of them, so this is print
  behaviour: large round fits print near nominal (about 0.3 of play across a 33 bore); small pin holes
  print undersize.
- **Fix, insert only** (the designer reuses his printed ring): `ins_grow` **0.10** grows the insert's body,
  flange and tongue radially, leaving 0.05 radial (measured on the meshes). The pins and their positions
  are unchanged. The arm mesh is byte-for-byte unchanged.
- **Fit coupons:** `part="insert_coupon"` is the flange, tongue, pins and first 3 mm of body as thin
  rings. `build_insert_coupons.py` → `out/insert_fit_coupons.3mf` at ins_grow 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.15
  (left to right, 3.9 g). `build_3mf.py` also writes `out/insert_only.3mf` (4.6 g).
- **Tested 2026-11-23 (the designer printed the 0.10 insert): "the new insert tolerances are great."**

## v17 (2026-11-23): GoPro fit tightened, plus fit coupons (the designer: "sloppy laterally")

- **Why it was sloppy:** our outer prongs are the arm's own walls, fixed top and bottom, so unlike a
  normal GoPro mount they can't flex in to take up play; the screw fights the whole arm. The fit has
  to come from the dimensions. Accessory: 2 prongs × 3.0 with a ~3.2 gap (9.2 across). v16:
  3.4 + 3.0 + 3.4 = 9.8, about 0.6 of play.
- **Now:** slots `gp_slot_w` **3.15**, tang `gp_web` **3.1** (9.4 across, ~0.2 nominal play, less as
  printed: top-down, the slots print a little narrow and the tang a little wide). The channel follows
  (9.4 wide); the nut recess has 1.8 to the slot.
- **Fit coupons:** `part="gopro_coupon"` is just the arm's GoPro station. `build_gopro_coupons.py` →
  `out/gopro_fit_coupons.3mf`: slot 3.05 / 3.15 / 3.25, left to right, printed top-down like the arm,
  5.4 g for all three. Pick the one that slides on by hand and grips with light screw pressure, then
  set `gp_slot_w` (and `gp_web` if the tang is what binds).
- **Tested 2026-11-23 (the designer): slot 3.15 is right** (tang 3.1). Of the three coupons it was the best
  fit. It's the default; the main .3mf already uses it.

## v14 (2026-11-23): two parts, no support: arm (printed top-down) + Wahoo insert pushed up through a ring

- **Why:** printed upright, the one-piece mount's whole underside sat on tree supports and came out
  rough (IMG_2600). Upside down, the 1 mm-proud seat holds the arm's top off the bed, and the
  receiver's floor and detent fingers would bridge ~30 mm. Vertical stands on the curved bar face.
- **Design (the designer):** the end of the arm is a **ring** (r 20.5, 41 across) with a bore. The **insert**
  (the Wahoo receiver, body r 16.6, 10 tall) is pushed **up from below**. Its flange (r 18.1, 1.6
  thick) bears 1.5 mm on the ring's underside, flush in a counterbore, so the computer's quarter-turn
  pulls the flange into the ring. Glue (epoxy) only locates it. Downward bump loads do go through the
  glue, but they're tiny (~15 N over ~1300 mm²).
- **Locating:** a tongue on the flange pointing **straight back** (4 mm, 10 wide) with **two d2.4
  pins** into holes in the arm, so the insert goes in square and can't turn. The receiver is 2-fold
  symmetric, so the orientation is right either way.
- **Flat underside** from the GoPro station straight across to the end of the ring (z 3.4); the
  bracket still slopes up from z 0 at the bar. Top edges have 0.6 45° chamfers (bed edges when printed
  top-down).
- **Checks:** arm/insert overlap 0; each part one body; insert floor min 3.35; bar contact 0.25.
  Down-facing area in print orientation: arm 48 mm² (hole tops, nut-recess bridge); insert: internal
  bridges over the finger relief and the receiver lip ledges. CLI slice with support OFF: 0% support,
  16.7 g, ~53 min.
- Clearances: bore 0.15 radial, counterbore 0.15, pin holes +0.3 dia. Not yet test-fitted.

## Print setup (2026-11-22, superseded by v14): upright, top face up, ironed

- **Orientation: upright, flat top up.** The show face (the arm's flat top and the raised seat) prints
  as whole top layers and is ironed. Upside down fails: the raised seat stands 1 mm proud, so the arm
  top can't lie on the bed, and the receiver's lip and tab slots would face down into support. On its
  side it rests on the disc's round edge, and the receiver's tabs would load across the layers.
- **`top_z` is snapped up to the 0.2 layer grid** (12.26 → **12.4**; seat 13.4, head underside 6.2), so
  the top faces are full layers. The skin over the bolt pocket grows to 1.74.
- **Slicer overrides:** ironing on top surfaces (10% flow, 0.15 spacing); top surface 120 mm/s; 6 top
  layers; monotonic top lines (profile default); `seam_position` back, with the part rotated on the bed
  so its bar end faces +Y, which puts the seam line on the hidden bar-contact face. Tree supports from
  the plate only: none can reach inside the receiver (its floor is in the way), the nut recess or the
  GoPro slots.
- **CLI slice (gcode features):** 13.8 g, ~56 min. Support + interface is 26% of the filament, all at
  z ≤ 7.4 (under the head's flat underside, the arm's underside and in the bolt pocket). Top surface
  and ironing reach the 12.4 and 13.4 faces. The receiver lip prints as an unsupported ~2.7 mm ledge.
- Material: the profile is PLA; pick PETG or ASA in the slicer for the bike.

### Contour gauge (2026-11-22): IMG_2583–2585, `section/trace_gauge.py`, `fit_gauge.py`

the designer pressed a printed contour gauge onto the bar front, held level. The photos have no rule.
- **Reading:** pins are segmented by hue, and each pin's tip comes from the outline's extreme
  point per pin-pitch window (IMG_2584, the contact end).
- **Fit:** scale and offset are fitted to the photo trace, with rotation fixed at 0 (pins level
  on the bar and straight across the photo).
- **Which way up is unresolved.** the designer said the photos are upside down. Both readings fit the
  trace to ~0.3 mm but need different pin pitches: **as photographed ~1.74 mm (1.47–1.95), flipped
  ~2.30 mm (1.90–2.48)**. Measuring the gauge's pin thickness settles it, and also lets the
  pins beyond the trace's range (above the top corner, below the nose) be used. Some of those
  pins clearly weren't on the bar: at either pitch the 33 pins span far more than the bar's 26 mm.
- **What it was used for (v6):** in the band the photo couldn't see (z 2.6–9.4), both readings
  agree to ~0.3 mm, so both feed the profile. `build_profile.py` now fits ONE smoothing spline
  x(z) to trace + gauge, with σ 0.12 mm: the stiffest that fits where the two sources agree
  without a curvature reversal (0.08 starts chasing pins). It removes the blend's kink at z 0. The

  surface at z 0, is now 36.9° nose-down (v5: 30.7°); its position is still a guess.
